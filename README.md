# Workforce Planning & HR Data Quality Analysis

## Executive Summary

A growing international education organisation required greater visibility into workforce composition, employee movement, contract risk, and underlying HR data quality issues affecting reporting confidence and workforce planning.

A workforce analytics solution was developed to monitor joiners and leavers, identify contract renewal risk, analyze workforce composition, and establish structured data validation controls. The analysis revealed significant concentrations of employee turnover within teaching roles, identified more than 200 fixed-term contracts due to expire within 90 days, and created a repeatable framework for improving data quality across HR reporting processes.

The resulting solution supports workforce planning, retention initiatives, and data-driven HR decision-making.

---

## Business Problem

HR leadership required answers to several critical workforce questions:

- How is the workforce changing over time?
- Which regions experience the highest employee movement?
- Which job families experience the highest levels of turnover?
- Which contracts are approaching expiration?
- How reliable is the workforce data used for reporting?
- Where should workforce planning efforts be prioritized?

Without a centralized reporting solution, answering these questions required significant manual effort and created inconsistencies across HR reporting.

To address these challenges, a workforce analytics and data quality reporting solution was developed.

## Key Findings

### Contract Renewal Risk Is Concentrated Within EMEA

Contract planning analysis identified 222 contracts expiring within the next 90 days.

Of these contracts:

- EMEA accounted for 157
- APAC accounted for 33
- Americas accounted for 32

### Why This Matters

Upcoming contract expirations create workforce planning risks and may lead to operational disruption if renewal decisions are delayed.

### Recommendation

Establish proactive contract review processes and prioritize workforce planning activities within EMEA.

---

### Teaching Roles Represent The Largest Contract Risk

The Teaching job family accounted for the largest concentration of contracts approaching expiration.

Other job families represented significantly smaller volumes of future contract risk.

### Why This Matters

Workforce stability within teaching positions has a disproportionate impact on business operations.

### Recommendation

Implement targeted workforce planning and succession activities focused on teaching positions.

---

### Workforce Turnover Is Concentrated Within Teaching Roles

Leavers analysis identified Teaching as the largest contributor to employee departures across the organization.

Other job families demonstrated substantially lower turnover levels.

### Why This Matters

High turnover within critical workforce groups can increase recruitment costs, impact continuity, and place pressure on operational teams.

### Recommendation

Conduct additional retention analysis to identify the drivers of employee departures within teaching roles.

---

### Permanent Employees Account For Most Workforce Exits

The majority of recorded employee departures occurred among permanent employees.

Fixed-term and casual employee departures represented significantly smaller proportions of total workforce exits.

### Why This Matters

Permanent employee turnover often carries greater replacement costs and operational impact.

### Recommendation

Strengthen retention initiatives for permanent employees and monitor workforce stability indicators more closely.

---

### Workforce Movement Differs Significantly By Region

Joiner and leaver activity varied across regions.

EMEA accounted for the largest volume of workforce movement, followed by the Americas and APAC.

### Why This Matters

Regional workforce planning requirements differ substantially and should not be managed using a single global approach.

### Recommendation

Develop region-specific workforce planning strategies aligned with local hiring and retention trends.

---

## Key Data Quality Findings

Validation checks identified issues including:

- Missing establishment codes
- CEO region mismatches
- Invalid contract end dates
- Incorrect employee type assignments
- Expatriate data inconsistencies
- Incorrect manager assignments
- Corporate email inconsistencies
- Country and location mismatches
- Employment status issues
- Working pattern validation failures

Each issue was documented and tracked through a dedicated issue management process.

---

## Data Quality Framework

The workforce validation process included:

1. Employee identifier reconciliation
2. Master data validation
3. Null value and consistency checks
4. Contract and date validation
5. Exception management and issue tracking

This process ensured that workforce reporting outputs were based on validated and reliable employee records.

### Business Implication

### Reporting Confidence Depends On Data Quality

### Business Implication
